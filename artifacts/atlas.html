Rev A was oversubscribed roughly two and a half times, which meant you would have dropped things at random rather than on purpose. The list is now four books, four pick-one pairs and four tool courses. Every nice-to-have you add back comes out of capstone time.

Sequence

The capstone starts in week three

It used to sit in month six, which left five months of input with no feedback — the way study plans quietly fail. It now runs the whole length and each phase lands in it: C4 and ADRs by week eight, a tenancy model by fourteen, billing by nineteen, a tested rollback by twenty-five. If you already have a real product in flight, use it; a live multi-tenant system beats a throwaway invoicing app.

Rule

Books for judgment, video for tools

Reversed from Rev A's start-with-a-course instinct. Architecture, data modelling, multi-tenancy and resilience are trade-off skills and they live in long-form writing. Docker, Terraform, CI/CD and cloud services are muscle memory and they live in video. The first three phases have almost no video in them on purpose.

Additions

Four topics the first draft was missing

Testing strategy — the largest gap, and it sits in phase 5. Cost per tenant, because an architect who cannot say what the worst tenant costs is missing half the job. Third-party integration architecture. And contract design for clients you cannot force-update, which is the phase 1 topic that bites hardest eighteen months later.

Evidence

Your phase numbers are already fiction

You have notes on load balancers and CDNs — phase 6 material — because one phase 0 course covered them. That is not a mistake, it is how the territory actually connects. Treat the phases as a syllabus to pull from, not a schedule to march through.

Gap

Reading is recognition; the sims are production

The phase sheets are for reading and self-testing — that is recognition-level work, and it is necessary. Nothing there makes you produce a design under constraints or live with a decision after you make it, which is what the Build Bench and Year One exist for. Do both or the reading will feel like progress it is not.

Cut list

What Rev B dropped, and why

The Pragmatic Programmer — you are past it. Clean Architecture — subsumed by Richards & Ford. Pogrebinsky's second course — overlaps the first. Alex Xu Vol. 2 — interview-oriented. Working Effectively with Legacy Code — a year-three book. Kubernetes demoted to vocabulary. And a Build-a-SaaS course, if you are already building one.

The week, rebuilt

Four hours, three of them fun
Mon – Thu

45 minutes of course or book. Paste the raw notes into the phase's _inbox.md. No decisions about where things go while you are studying — that part of your system already works.

When the inbox fills

Ask Claude to distribute it. The notes become concept files and get merged into this phase's sheet, which is republished at the same link. You never have to open a note to review it.

One evening

One commission on the Build Bench for the phase you are in. Fifteen minutes. If the review comes back rejected, the requirements it flagged tell you what to read next — a far better reading list than the one you wrote in advance.

Sunday · 20 min

Open your phase sheet and take the blank page. Pick a concept, write it from memory, then reveal its cards and diff. Anything you could not produce gets marked shaky here on the map.

Sunday · 10 min

Play Year One. Ten minutes, seventeen decisions, and it ends by telling you which principles you paid for. Those go on next week's list.

Per phase

Come back here and re-mark the map. Anything still amber after a phase closes is the thing to over-study; anything green early can be left alone for a month.
